Ageless Wisdom

A day in the life of one man, lived three times across three thousand years

Paa Sankar August 2026 12 min read

Wisdom doesn't age. Worry just finds better shoes.

Three men are about to have the same day. One works a stretch of red earth and irrigation tanks in 1000 BCE, in a village that will, in time, be called Tamil Nadu. One sits at a desk on the same red earth in 2026, several thousand years and one air conditioner later. One is several planets away in 2100, tied to both by blood and by a nickname that used to be a full name.

Call them Ramasamy Chettiar, Raman C, and RC. Same lineage. Same worries, mostly. Different stationery.

5:00 AM

Waking

1000 BCE · Ramasamy Chettiar

Ramasamy wakes when the sky does. There is no other instrument for it — the rooster two huts down, the first grey light through the thatch, the smell of the previous night's fire gone cold. His first thought is the sky itself: will it hold rain, or won't it. Everything today depends on an answer he will only get by watching.

2026 · Raman C

Raman wakes to a phone already lit, three notifications deep before his eyes have properly opened. He checks them lying down, in the dark, before he has decided whether he is actually awake. The sky, if he thinks of it at all, is something the weather app has already summarised for him.

2100 · RC

RC wakes to a habitat cycling slowly from blue-black to a manufactured dawn, calibrated to a circadian rhythm that no longer has a real sun to answer to. His first read isn't the sky — there is no sky, only a viewport — but the transit manifest, and a flashing line item on the Andromeda colony funding round that has moved overnight while he slept across two time zones and one gravitational field.

1000 BCE · Ramasamy Chettiar

Ramasamy's walk to the tank bund is his exercise and his labour at once — has always been the same thing, needs no separate name. This morning a trader who came through overnight has left behind a harder rumour than usual: the king two rivers east has been counting his own granaries, finding them short, and has started, it's said, looking rather too closely at everyone else's.

2026 · Raman C

Raman's gym has three televisions on, none of them muted. Between sets he half-watches one — the US and Iran again, an anchor doing the thing anchors do with a chart and a red arrow, oil climbing while the ticker below it climbs with it. He finishes his reps unsure whether his pulse is up from the exercise or the headline.

2100 · RC

RC doesn't so much exercise as get exercised — a chair that flexes and resists on his behalf while he lies back and supplies none of the effort himself. The feed behind his eyes this morning carries word that Mars is restless again, some faction there talking openly of throttling the water allocation to Saturn, and the settlements on three of its moons are already rationing on the rumour alone.

10:00 AM

The day multiplies

Time didn't speed up. Only the number of things trying to fit inside it did.

1000 BCE · Ramasamy Chettiar

Ramasamy is already out along the tank bund, doing the morning's rounds on foot. There are four things on his mind, and he can hold all four at once without effort: a breach in the irrigation channel that needs the headman's attention, a dispute over a strayed bullock between two households, the state of the grain stored from the last harvest, and a trader from two villages over who is due, possibly, if the roads have held.

Before he can sit for his meal, word comes down river: the spice shipment from the coast is delayed again, the pirates in the strait wanting a fresh price before letting it pass. The fourth time in ten years. Ramasamy sends a messenger inland at once, asking the king to deal with it strictly this time, and not merely send back a promise, as he did the last three.

2026 · Raman C

Raman is at his desk with three tabs open, a client call running in one ear, a market index ticking in the corner of his eye, and a half-written email he keeps returning to and abandoning. He would say he is doing many things. He is mostly being interrupted by many things, one after another, none of them finished.

Raman's phone buzzes before lunch with something worse: the factory in Raichur has gone on strike, negotiations with the union broken down overnight. The third factory this year. He types himself a note — talk to the CM about this, properly, this week — and closes the app before he has to look at it any longer.

2100 · RC

RC is coordinating a supply run from Mars while a Saturn shipment sits delayed behind a communications lag he can't shorten no matter how many times he refreshes the feed. By his own count he is handling perhaps a hundred things this morning — or so he would think, if he ever stopped moving long enough to count them properly, which he does not.

Just before he would have eaten, if he still properly ate, a red flag reaches RC: the water shipment is delayed at the unloading port, a technical fault, and every minute it sits there is bleeding credits he can watch draining in real time. The third time this cycle the AI preventive-maintenance system has failed to catch a fault before it became one. He posts a note to his assistant — draft something for the solar system governor, have it ready by end of day — and goes back to watching the counter fall.

1:00 PM

What sustains

1000 BCE · Ramasamy Chettiar

Ramasamy's meal is also his labour. Millet, ground that morning on a stone quern by his own hand, cooked over a wood fire, eaten with his fingers off a broad leaf, with whatever greens and pulses the season allows. Rice is something he has heard of, grown a little further off — a newer, fussier crop that hasn't quite won his household over yet. After the meal comes betel leaf and areca nut, chewed slow, and a private, familiar grievance against the vaidyar who has told him, again, to eat less of everything on account of his girth. He chews anyway. A man who can afford to be portly, he reasons, has earned the right to argue with his physician.

His granddaughter finds Ramasamy just after the meal, and for the better part of an hour the day stops making demands of him. He tells her the story she always wants — the one about the river and the clever fox, most likely — and she corrects him the moment he strays, the way she always does. It must happen exactly the same way each time, or it isn't the story.

For a few minutes after lunch, before the day picks him back up, Ramasamy's mind drifts to the old stories about the people who came before farming — who followed the herds and the fruiting trees, and answered to nothing but the next meal. No tanks to maintain, no bullocks to arbitrate, no grain to count. It sounds, in this quiet stretch, wonderfully simple. He has no way of knowing he may be wrong about that.

2026 · Raman C

Raman eats at his desk, screen still glowing, the meal barely registering between one message and the next. His cardiologist has been very clear about salt, about ghee, about the pickle jar specifically. Raman reaches for the pickle jar specifically, and mutters something uncharitable about doctors who have clearly never had a deadline.

Raman fits in a quick video call with his fiancée between meetings — a few minutes to exchange notes on how the day's gone so far, and to confirm, again, that Saturday's movie is still on. It is a personal call wearing the shape of a status update.

Raman, in the gap between his fiancée's call and the next meeting, half-watches something on his phone about slow living, and pictures — vaguely, generously — a life spent under a tree, watching the grass grow, answerable to no one. It is a life that has never existed quite the way he's imagining it, but the imagining is restful, so he keeps it — right up until the next notification arrives.

2100 · RC

RC doesn't really eat. A nutrient bar, dissolved and optimised, consumed somewhere between one gravity well and the next, chased by a pill his ship's medic-AI did not authorise and he took anyway. The one thing he still chews is a toothpick — a habit he can't quite explain the origin of, except that it seems to have replaced something. It has: it is what remains, two generations on, of Raman's tobacco, worn down by time into its most harmless possible shape, the compulsion outliving every form it has ever worn.

RC's call with his current planet partner is shorter still, and it isn't really about either of them: the planetary governor wants him at a virtual party back on Earth, and the two of them need to work out, quickly, whether that's manageable around everything else this week. Nothing in the conversation would sound out of place if it had been a call about the water shipment instead.

RC, in the brief lull after his own call, envies, for a moment, a life lived on one planet a day — not shuttled between Earth and Mars and Saturn before breakfast, not required to hold a colony's books and a candidate's chances in the same conversation. It doesn't last. The console chimes, and the moment is gone before he's finished having it.

6:00 PM

The turn

1000 BCE · Ramasamy Chettiar

Just before he can close for the day, a delegation rides in from the capital — kingsmen sent ahead of the rumoured war, asking for a contribution toward the effort. What starts as a demand turns, by the time the sun is low, into a negotiation: his support now, in exchange for standing trade concessions once matters settle. He agrees. By the time the delegation rides off, it has been, against everything the morning suggested, a profitable day.

Ramasamy closes the day's trade at the post by the tank, walks home as the light actually fades, and lets the day end because the sun has ended it for him. There is no lamp bright enough in his hut to argue with the dark.

2026 · Raman C

The client call that nearly fell apart at lunch reconvenes at five, and by six it has not only survived but closed — a longer contract, concessions that will cost Raman next quarter's numbers and, if he's read it right, set the business up properly for the five years after that. He signs off feeling, for the first time all day, like the day actually went somewhere.

Raman's day does not really end at six. It thins out, but it doesn't stop — one more message, one more "just this last thing" that turns into three more. The light outside has nothing to do with it any more.

2100 · RC

Just before he'd normally sign off, a call reaches RC from an exploratory mining engineer three systems out — a genuine strike, an asteroid with a core worth staking a claim on immediately, before a competitor's survey teams get anywhere near it. He spends the last working hour of the day moving fast — filing, securing, claiming. By any measure he keeps, it is a satisfying day.

RC has, since this morning, crossed a planet and possibly a time zone that doesn't have a name yet. Six o'clock is a number on a console, not a fact about the sky. He has been awake for what his body insists was one day and what the calendar insists was rather more complicated than that.

Night

The day ends, almost

1000 BCE · Ramasamy Chettiar

Ramasamy's night starts early — fruit, a lamp lit low, and a quiet hour with his wife catching up on the village's real news: who visited whom, whose cattle wandered off, what the potter's wife had to say about the trader's prices this time.

2026 · Raman C

Raman stops at the bar on his way home, one drink, just enough to unwind before the next thing starts. Then the industry association dinner — under the small talk, the real conversation is about whether he should stand for the association presidency this cycle, the kind of seat that buys a business more leverage with the ministry than any quarter's numbers could on their own.

2100 · RC

RC's evening starts with a quick pass through the health centre — a scan, a supplement adjustment, ten minutes he can't honestly call rest but counts as such anyway. Then a virtual dinner with a small circle of political friends, mostly to gauge which way the wind is blowing for their preferred candidate's run at the planetary council — and, quietly, the way these things are always mentioned quietly, to remember what was promised in exchange for the right kind of support before the vote.

Same file, different stationery

In bed, each of them gives thanks first, then asks. Ramasamy thanks whichever god is listening for the day's deal, and asks for more of the same — for the concessions to hold, for opportunities like today's to keep finding him, and, if the gods are feeling generous, for the day he might be spoken of as the kingpin of trade across the whole kingdom, not merely his corner of it. Raman thanks god for a productive day, and asks — as he does most nights — for a push toward the president's chair at the association. It feels, some nights, so near. Tonight it feels rather further than that. RC thanks god for a profitable day, and asks for one thing more: that the wind keeps blowing his candidate's way, and the council seat lands where it needs to.

Three requests. Three different vocabularies. All of them, underneath, asking for the same thing — more of today, and a permanent claim on the power to keep making it happen.

They land, all three, in the same place at once — because where they're going, there was never any distance between 1000 BCE and 2100 to begin with. God reads them the way He reads all of it: everything, always, arriving together. He turns to the assistant keeping the log.

"Same file," He says. "Different stationery." The assistant doesn't look up. "Noted, Sir. That's the 1,342,567,984th time we've asked for more instead of sitting with what we already have. Tomorrow will make it 1,342,567,985."

"Permission to open a new file, Sir — species v21, same intelligence, upgraded for actually being satisfied with what it's given?" He does not answer. The assistant logs the silence as well, and gets back to the queue.
